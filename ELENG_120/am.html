Additionally, if we have phase drift, then our output could look something like this:

\begin{align} r(t) &= x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)\cos(\omega_0 t + \theta) \notag \\ &= \frac{1}{2}x(t)\left[\cos(2\omega_0 t + \theta)+\cos \theta\right] \notag \end{align}

If we put this through a low-pass filter with gain \(2\), then we get at the output \(x(t)\cos\theta\). The biggest problem arises when our phase is offset by exactly \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\): in this case, we get zero as our output signal!

Thus, we need some other demodulating techniques.
